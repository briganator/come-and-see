The Book

Come and See

The story of Joseph Smith and the Restoration

“Nathanael said unto him, Can there any good thing come out of Nazareth? Philip saith unto him, Come and see.”

John 1:46

In 1535 a prisoner in a cold cell asked for a candle, “for it is wearisome to sit alone in the dark.” In 1820 a fourteen-year-old farm boy walked into the woods to ask God which church was right, and came out saying he had seen a light brighter than the sun. This is the story of that boy, of the family that raised him, and of what his answer cost. It is told as a narrative, from the records the people themselves left: Lucy Mack Smith's history, Joseph Smith's own accounts, letters, journals, and the Church's published histories. Where a scene detail is imagined, the book says so with a small mark (†). Scene details that rest on a source have a note. Atmospheric touches (weather, light, sound, small gestures) are marked † too, and they come from no source. The facts, dates, quotations and dialogue come from documented sources, cited in the notes.

It is written for Latter-day Saints, for friends of other faiths, and with special tenderness for Jewish readers, whose prophets Elijah and Malachi run through these pages. No one is asked to agree. The invitation is Philip's: come and see.

Lucy and Joseph

A wedding gift, a lost fortune, a covenant made on a sickbed, and a son born in Sharon, Vermont.

The Boy Who Would Not Be Bound

A boy who would not be bound, a year without a summer, and a mother who brought her children west.

V

A Pillar of Light

A verse in James, a grove of trees, and a pillar of light in the spring of 1820.

VI
Chapter Six

The Last Words of Malachi

An angel quotes Malachi, Alvin dies, Joseph marries Emma, and the plates are entrusted to him.

VII
Chapter Seven

By the Gift and Power of God

Lost pages, a schoolteacher scribe, priesthood by the river, witnesses, and five thousand copies.

VIII
Chapter Eight

Come and See

A church organized in a log house, a knapsack of books, and a gathering to Ohio.

The Lord's House

The Refiner's Fire

A City Beautiful

Carthage, and the Work Rolls On
