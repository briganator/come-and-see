Primary Sources &amp; First Accounts

The book is built on records the people themselves left. These public collections let you read them, most in images of the original pages.

  • The Joseph Smith Papers

    The complete documentary edition of Joseph Smith’s papers: images and transcripts of the original manuscripts.

  • Joseph Smith, History, circa Summer 1832

    The earliest surviving account of the First Vision, partly in Joseph’s own hand.

  • Joseph Smith, History, 1838–1856, vol. A-1

    The history begun in 1838, the source of the account canonized as Joseph Smith—History.

  • Joseph Smith—History (Pearl of Great Price)

    The canonized excerpt of Joseph’s 1838 account.

  • First Vision Accounts (Gospel Topics essay)

    Compares the accounts Joseph gave and those recorded by others.

  • Lucy Mack Smith, History, 1844–1845

    Joseph’s mother’s own history of the family, the main source for Chapters 2–4.

  • Book of Mormon, 1830 edition

    Page images of the first edition printed by E. B. Grandin in Palmyra.

  • Testimony of Three Witnesses

    The witnesses’ statement, printed with the Book of Mormon since 1830.

  • Testimony of Eight Witnesses

    The second witness statement.

  • Journal, 1835–1836

    Joseph’s Kirtland journal, including the temple dedication and the visions of April 3, 1836.

  • Doctrine and Covenants 110

    The visions in the Kirtland Temple, including Elijah.

  • Letter to the Church and Edward Partridge, 20 March 1839

    Written from Liberty Jail; portions became Doctrine and Covenants 121–123.

  • Saints, vol. 1: The Standard of Truth

    The Church’s narrative history, 1815–1846.

  • Church History Topics

    Short reference articles on people, places and events.

  • Church History Library catalog

    Search and view original journals, letters and photographs.

  • BYU Studies

    Peer-reviewed scholarship on Latter-day Saint history.

Each chapter also lists its own sources in its endnotes.
