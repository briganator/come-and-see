Study Guides

One short guide per chapter: a summary, two questions to talk about, and scriptures to read together.

1. A Famine of Hearing

From Tyndale's cell to a Passover table and a Vermont farmhouse: the long wait for heaven to speak again.

  1. Why did people like Tyndale risk their lives to put the Bible in plain language?
  2. Amos foretold “a famine … of hearing the words of the LORD.” What would it mean for heaven to be silent?

Read: Amos 8:11–12; Malachi 4:5–6

Read the chapter · Listen

2. Seeds in Stony Ground

Solomon and Lydia Mack, Asael and Mary Smith: two plain New England families and the seeds they planted.

  1. What did Solomon and Lydia Mack and Asael and Mary Smith pass on to their children?
  2. What faith or habit would you like to pass on in your own family?

Read the chapter · Listen

3. Lucy and Joseph

A wedding gift, a lost fortune, a covenant made on a sickbed, and a son born in Sharon, Vermont.

  1. How did Joseph Sr. and Lucy handle the loss of their money and the debts that followed?
  2. What did Lucy promise God during her illness, and how did she keep looking?

Read the chapter · Listen

4. The Boy Who Would Not Be Bound

A boy who would not be bound, a year without a summer, and a mother who brought her children west.

  1. What does Joseph’s leg surgery show about him and about his parents?
  2. How did the family keep going through hard years and moves?

Read the chapter · Listen

5. A Pillar of Light

A verse in James, a grove of trees, and a pillar of light in the spring of 1820.

  1. What question did Joseph take into the grove, and what led him to ask it?
  2. When have you asked God a sincere question?

Read: James 1:5; Joseph Smith—History 1:10–20

Read the chapter · Listen

6. The Last Words of Malachi

An angel quotes Malachi, Alvin dies, Joseph marries Emma, and the plates are entrusted to him.

  1. Why do you think Moroni quoted Malachi’s promise about Elijah?
  2. How did Joseph grow during the four years before he received the plates?

Read: Malachi 4:5–6; Joseph Smith—History 1:36–39

Read the chapter · Listen

7. By the Gift and Power of God

Lost pages, a schoolteacher scribe, priesthood by the river, witnesses, and five thousand copies.

  1. What did Joseph learn from the loss of the 116 pages?
  2. Why do the witnesses matter?

Read: Doctrine and Covenants 13; Testimony of Three Witnesses

Read the chapter · Listen

8. Come and See

A church organized in a log house, a knapsack of books, and a gathering to Ohio.

  1. What happened on April 6, 1830, and why was it small and simple?
  2. Why did so many choose to gather to Ohio?

Read: Doctrine and Covenants 21

Read the chapter · Listen

9. The Lord's House

The Kirtland Temple is built, dedicated, and visited by Elijah at Passover in 1836.

  1. What did the Saints give up to build the Kirtland Temple?
  2. Why is Elijah’s coming at Passover in 1836 meaningful to Jews and Latter-day Saints alike?

Read: Doctrine and Covenants 109–110

Read the chapter · Listen

10. The Refiner's Fire

Kirtland's trials, the Missouri war, the ice of the Mississippi, and the jail at Liberty.

  1. What comfort did Joseph receive in Liberty Jail?
  2. How can hard experiences “give thee experience”?

Read: Doctrine and Covenants 121–122

Read the chapter · Listen

11. A City Beautiful

Nauvoo rises from a swamp: healing, baptism for the dead, the Relief Society, and a prayer in Jerusalem.

  1. How did the Saints turn a swamp into a city?
  2. Why was the Relief Society important then, and now?

Read: Doctrine and Covenants 124

Read the chapter · Listen

12. Carthage, and the Work Rolls On

Carthage, the martyrdom, and a work that rolled on.

  1. How did the Saints carry on after the deaths of Joseph and Hyrum?
  2. What part of this story will you remember most?

Read: Doctrine and Covenants 135

Read the chapter · Listen
